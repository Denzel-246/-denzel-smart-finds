Best Laptops for Students: What to Look For Before Buying

The specs that actually matter for lectures, essays, and light coding — and the ones you can safely ignore.

Start with what you'll actually do on it

Before comparing specs, write down the three things you'll do most: note-taking and browsing, writing and spreadsheets, or something heavier like video editing or coding. Most coursework — essays, slides, browser tabs, video calls — doesn't need a powerful machine. Save the budget for battery life and a good keyboard instead.

The specs worth prioritizing

  • RAM: 8GB is a workable minimum in 2026; 16GB is more comfortable if you multitask heavily or plan to keep the laptop for several years.
  • Storage: An SSD (not a traditional hard drive) makes a bigger day-to-day difference than almost any other spec. 256GB is fine if you rely on cloud storage; 512GB gives more breathing room.
  • Battery life: Look for a laptop rated for 8+ hours of real-world use, not just the marketing number.
  • Weight: If you're carrying it to lectures daily, anything over about 1.6kg starts to feel heavy over a semester.

What matters less than people think

Processor generation numbers and marketing names change every year and are hard to compare directly. Unless you're doing heavy video editing, 3D work, or serious coding, a mid-range current-generation processor is plenty — don't overspend chasing the newest chip.

Before you buy

Check the return policy, check whether the specific configuration (not just the model name) has the RAM and storage you want, and compare the same configuration across retailers rather than comparing different configurations at different prices.
