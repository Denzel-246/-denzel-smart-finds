Best Productivity Tools for Remote Workers

Software and hardware that make working from anywhere less chaotic.

A second screen changes more than people expect

Even a small external monitor reduces the constant window-switching that eats into focus time — commonly cited as one of the highest-impact, lowest-cost home office upgrades.

A dedicated task list

Whether it's a notebook or an app, keeping one place for tasks (instead of scattered sticky notes and chat messages) reduces the mental overhead of remembering what's next.

A comfortable, quiet headset

For anyone on frequent video calls, a headset with a clear microphone matters more for professionalism than most people budget for.

Boundaries between "work" and "home"

Simple physical cues — closing a laptop, changing rooms, or a consistent shutdown routine — help remote workers mentally close the workday, which is harder without a commute to mark the transition.
